Why public art matters

Public art across the UK

Towns and cities across the country are investing in murals and street art. The examples below are other organisations' projects, not ours. We share them as useful context for anyone considering a commission.

Preston city centre

A council-backed programme is bringing six large murals to walls and buildings that previously stood empty, at sites including Fishergate, Friargate, Lune Street, Lancaster Road and the Flag Market. The works are planned as a mural trail, and it is hoped they will encourage landlords to improve their own frontages.

Read the news report on Central Radio (opens in a new tab)

Upfest, Bristol

Upfest began in 2008 and is described as Europe's biggest street-art festival. It now involves more than 250 artists and draws about 80,000 visitors over 17 days.

Visit the Upfest website (opens in a new tab)

Street art as a visitor attraction

VisitBritain promotes street art as a reason to visit cities including Bristol, London, Leicester, Glasgow, Manchester, Birmingham and Edinburgh.

Read the VisitBritain guide (opens in a new tab)

Community RePaint

This UK network redistributes leftover paint to community projects, including murals. It reports collecting more than 486,000 litres in 2023. Where it suits the project, we can source reused paint as a more sustainable option.

Visit the Community RePaint website (opens in a new tab)
